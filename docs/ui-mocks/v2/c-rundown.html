Spotify Playlist Mixer

Blend several playlists into one.

Pick up to ten of your Spotify playlists, say how much of each you want and how many songs should play in a row, then save the result as a new playlist. You can reorder the tracks before you save.

  • Choose playlistsSearch your library or paste a Spotify link.
  • Set the blendDrag the dividers to give each playlist its share, by play time or by song count.
  • Check the running order and saveDrag tracks into place. Start times update as you go.

The app asks to read your playlists and to create new ones. It does not post anything to your profile.

What you get

A running order with start times, built from the blend you choose. This one is an example.

        Design notes

        Palette

        #FFFFFF paper #111111 ink #FFF200 highlighter #D62828 red #003049 navy #1B7F73 teal #946200 amber

        Three inks mark the three selected sources. Amber, olive (#5E6B00) and grey (#4A4A4A) are held back for the extra playlists. The highlighter marks selection and nothing else.

        Fonts

        Public Sans for all text. IBM Plex Mono for the start and length columns of the running order only, so the times line up like a programme log.

        Trade-off

        Plainness over delight. There is no cover art in the order rows and the left column runs long on desktop, but every row is a ruled line you can scan like a printed sheet.

        Three signature ideas

        1. Start times are cumulative and recompute while you drag a row, so reordering shows its effect on the clock immediately.
        2. One highlighter colour, used only for what is selected: the chosen balance method, preset, length option, the current row and the divider you are holding.
        3. The blend is a bar with draggable dividers, a plain table of numbers and one line of coloured letters. No cards anywhere.

        Tells avoided and how

        Flat white page, hairline rules and 3px corners instead of colour ramps, glow, blur and shadows; one text face plus a mono for numbers; plain h2 headings with no numbering, badges or masthead; presets as a ruled list; totals as a sentence; every size at 13px or above.
